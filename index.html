Research

AI Gatekeeping and Technographic Power: Longitudinal and Cross-National Evidence from Large Language Models

Research Assistant, March 2026 to present. Authors: Jun Luo, Jiazhi Xie, Xinqing Li.

Status: abstract accepted at two conferences in 2026 (Artificial Intelligence, Communication, and Social Transformation, Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University; the 12th International Journal of Press/Politics Conference, Singapore). Not yet published.

I took part in design discussions: which models to sample, which topics to compare across countries, and whether to build our own coding framework or adapt a gatekeeping-theory taxonomy written for human editors. I reviewed about 40 related papers on gatekeeping theory, large language models, political bias and methodology; I coded more than 800 LLM-generated paragraphs for categorical and ideological patterns; and I summarized output differences between models.

Preliminary observations from my hand-coding: outputs on American crime topics default to a narrow script of guns, domestic violence, and criminal networks, almost regardless of the case; outputs on Chinese political topics default to the government as the narrative actor, almost regardless of who the story is about. Metrics such as cross-entropy can measure such skews, but explaining what they mean for a culture's default script is still mostly manual work.
